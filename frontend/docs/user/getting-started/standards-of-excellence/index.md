Linux Foundation mentoring programs grow future open source contributors and leaders. Everyone taking part is expected to be respectful, collaborative and professional. These Standards set out what is encouraged and what is not acceptable.

## Who they apply to

The Standards apply to every mentor, mentee and candidate in a mentoring program the Linux Foundation runs, including LFX Mentorship and Google Summer of Code. They cover what you do in a Linux Foundation context, and anything that could affect one of its mentoring programs.

You must also follow the Code of Conduct of the project your mentorship is with, and the Linux Foundation's other policies. Program administrators can remove offensive content, disqualify a participant, or take any other action needed to uphold the Standards.

## Report a concern

Send any evidence, such as screenshots, with your report.

- **A breach of these Standards** — email [mentorship@linuxfoundation.org](mailto:mentorship@linuxfoundation.org).
- **Harassment** — use the reporting process in the project's own Code of Conduct.

## Respect volunteers' time

Mentors and project teams are volunteers, and each program receives far more applications than it can accept.

- **No individual feedback on applications.** Mentors and program staff cannot explain why a particular application was not selected.
- **Selection decisions are final.** Do not ask for a decision to be reconsidered, demand feedback, or keep asking about future openings after you have been declined.
- **Use the right channels.** You are welcome to suggest improvements and to talk about your experience on your own social media. Do not mass-email project teams or staff, and do not use project or program mailing lists, Slack channels or issue trackers to dispute a selection. Doing so can get you blocked or disqualified.

## Collaborate and support others

Whether you are a mentor, mentee or candidate, help others learn:

- Thank people and recognize them when they help.
- Praise in public; give negative or awkward feedback in private.
- Give constructive, useful feedback on other people's pull requests.
- Own your mistakes, and be understanding about other people's.

## Communicate with respect

Candid discussion and disagreement are welcome, but never an excuse for bad behavior. Rude, offensive, disparaging or threatening statements, including insults and degrading language, are not allowed.

## Accept evaluations gracefully

Mentors carry out the midterm and final evaluations against the project requirements agreed at the start. Their evaluations are final. You may ask clarifying questions, but drawn-out disputes about an evaluation, or pressing a mentor to change one, are not acceptable.

## Prohibited behavior

Illegal, deceptive, abusive or disruptive behavior is not tolerated. This includes, but is not limited to, the following.

### Gaming metrics

Sincere small fixes are welcome. Submitting large numbers of superficial, trivial or AI-generated pull requests to pad your numbers is not. It wastes reviewers' time, and it can lead to disqualification.

### Zero tolerance

These lead to **immediate disqualification**:

- **Deception** — falsifying your identity, location or experience, or anything else that undermines the program or its selection process.
- **Harassing mentors and project teams** — repeatedly pinging, messaging or badgering them for a review or an update on your application.
- **Retaliation and threats** — threatening project teams or program staff over a selection or any other program decision.
- **Toxic competition** — publicly disparaging other candidates' work, or harassing people to put them off applying.

## Changes to the Standards

The Linux Foundation may change these Standards at any time. Active participants are told about major changes by email. A participant who does not agree to an updated version can no longer take part.
