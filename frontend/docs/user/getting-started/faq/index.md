## Is there a stipend?

Some LFX Mentorship programs offer a stipend and some do not. Where a stipend is offered it varies by program and by your country of residence, and the program page states it before you apply. See [Stipends](../../stipends/).

## Who can apply?

Anyone who is 18 or older by the time the program starts, is eligible to work in the country they live in for the duration of the mentorship, and is not taking part in, or has not completed, another Linux Foundation mentorship program. You must also take part as an individual, with no obligation such as an employer contract that stops you, and must not live where applicable law prohibits taking part. See **Who can apply** in [Getting Started](../) for the full list.

## Do I need to be a student?

You do not need to be a student. Some programs ask for school enrollment verification as a prerequisite, but many do not. The program page lists the prerequisites.

## How many programs can I apply to?

You can have up to three applications open at a time. Withdrawing an application frees a slot.

## How much time does a term take?

A significant weekly commitment for the whole term. A full-time mentorship usually runs for 12 weeks at about 40 hours a week, and a part-time one for 24 weeks at about 20 hours a week. The Program Admin sets whether you are full time or part time when they accept you. Each program page lists its own term dates.

## When do programs run?

Each program sets its own term dates, shown on its program page. Many follow the Linux Foundation's usual schedule:

| Term       | Runs                       |
| ---------- | -------------------------- |
| **Spring** | March 1 to May 31          |
| **Summer** | June 1 to August 31        |
| **Fall**   | September 1 to November 30 |

Programs on this schedule are usually listed about six weeks before the term starts. They take applications for about four weeks and choose their mentees in the two weeks before the term begins.

## How are applicants chosen?

The program's mentors and Program Admins review each application against the skills the program lists and the prerequisite tasks you submitted. Mentors may also interview candidates. The Program Admin then accepts applicants for the term.

Selection decisions are final. Programs receive many more applications than they can accept, so they cannot tell you why an application was not selected. See the [Standards of Excellence](../standards-of-excellence/).

## What happens at the end of a term?

You complete a final evaluation with your mentor. Once the Program Admin marks you as graduated, you receive a letter by email from the Linux Foundation confirming that you completed the program, and graduating is recorded on your public mentee profile.

## Are mentees employed by the Linux Foundation?

No, mentees are not employees of the Linux Foundation, and anything agreed between a mentee and a company is outside the program. For jobs at the Linux Foundation, see [linuxfoundation.org/about/careers](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/about/careers).

## How do I get help?

Select **Contact support** at the bottom of this site. Support chat is in LFX Self Serve, so sign in there and use the chat button. You can also email [mentorship@linuxfoundation.org](mailto:mentorship@linuxfoundation.org).

To report a breach of the [Standards of Excellence](../standards-of-excellence/), email the same address. Report harassment through the project's own Code of Conduct process.

## Do I need an account to browse?

You do not need an account to browse. Programs, mentees and mentors are all public. You need an [LF account](https://openprofile.dev/) to apply, to mentor, or to administer a program.

## Where do I actually apply?

Applying happens in LFX, the Linux Foundation's software platform, not on this site. **Apply to This Program** on a program page takes you to [app.lfx.dev](https://app.lfx.dev). See [Apply to a Program](../../mentees/apply-to-a-program/).
